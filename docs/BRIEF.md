## Vision and pillars

Wahala is a raw, 18+ browser game where you date a pre-built Lagos character for 7 in-game days, survive the wahala, and walk away with a savage Verdict Card you can't help posting. Lagos Life made all of Lagos life the game; we take the one part of life everyone obsesses over, dating, and make everything else in your life bend around it.

Five pillars guide every decision:

1. **Relationship is the spine.** Money, job, looks, friends and reputation all exist to feed or wreck the relationship. Work shows up only when it collides with love.
2. **Same character, different story.** Two Sugar Babes dating Chief Emeka at the same minute get different truths, different cards, different dice and different endings. Spoilers become debates.
3. **Every run ends in a share.** A run takes 3 to 5 minutes and always ends in a screenshot-ready card that roasts you against your own secret goal.
4. **Raw where it has to be raw.** Messy, brutal, funny, exaggerated real life. No moralising for its own sake; a lesson only lands when the player earned it.
5. **Learn without knowing.** The card reveals the red flags you missed. Players laugh, then think, "wait, my real guy did this." That is why they share and why they replay.

## Player setup

Setup takes under 45 seconds across five screens, then the player is on a first date.

1. **Age gate.** "Wahala: Lagos is an 18+ interactive story game with romance, secrets and choices that have consequences. Are you 18 or older?" Yes enters; No shows "Wahala High is coming. Follow us for news." with social links only. Never collect names, numbers or emails on the No path.
2. **Who you are.** Man, Woman, or Non-binary. Pick a display name (shown in the Parlour) and a simple avatar (see Avatar and hidden Chemistry). Avatar has a hidden Glow level (1 to 5) that the Clinic raises.
3. **Who you date.** Men, Women, or Both. This filters the roster.
4. **Your vibe.** Sets starting Wallet, Sanity, Clout, your job, and how every character first reacts to you.
5. **Your secret goal.** Never shown to characters, only judged on your Verdict Card.

### Avatar and hidden Chemistry

The avatar is three quick taps, and each tap is secretly scored against the partner's hidden Taste.

| Tap | Options (women) | Options (men) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Build | Slim and Trim · Petite Baddie · Curvy / Thick · Athletic · Soft and Full | Lean and Clean · Gym Rat · Big Daddy · Average Guy · Tall and Slim |
| Hair | 30-inch Bone Straight · Knotless Braids · Natural / Low Cut · Coloured Frontal · Locs | Low Cut · Waves · Locs · Clean Bald · Beard Gang |
| Style | Designer Glam · Alté / Streetwear · Smart Casual · Owambe Traditional | Designer Drip · Alté / Streetwear · Smart Casual · Agbada / Senator |

Every character has a hidden Taste profile: two fixed favourites (Chief always likes Curvy and Bone Straight) plus one favourite rolled per run. A match adds hidden **Chemistry**, which boosts Bond gains and gift sizes.

- The player sees only "+5 Chemistry" or "+2 Chemistry", never why. The mystery is the point: players theorise in the Parlour and on X.
- Occasional leaks hint at the reason: "He keeps looking at your shoes." "Why are you dressed like my nephew who makes TikToks?"
- Every build, hair and style option is a favourite of at least one character, so no avatar is simply worse. Wildcard Spark: about 1 in 6 runs, the partner falls for the player regardless of Taste, because people date outside their type.
- Upkeep matters: the Glow Clinic and gym keep Chemistry up; skipping upkeep on a high-maintenance partner slowly drains it.

### Date prep

Outfits are chosen inside pre-date cards, not a separate shop: splurge (Wallet down, Clout up, big reward if it matches Taste), play it safe (free, partner may lose interest), or borrow from your Bestie (free, 30% chance of a disaster). A full shop arrives after launch.

## Meters and hidden state

Three meters are always on screen; four values stay hidden and only surface on the Verdict Card.

| Meter | Range | Visible | What moves it | At zero |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wallet (₦) | 0 and up | Yes | Gifts, transfers, clinic, bribes, tabs, salary | Sapa Eviction ending (Bail-Out offered) |
| Sanity (%) | 0 to 100 | Yes | Lies, gaslighting, public scenes, Bestie, Therapist | Public Breakdown ending (Bail-Out offered) |
| Street Clout (%) | 0 to 100 | Yes | Being seen, posting, scandals, glow-ups | No game over; low Clout locks some choices |
| Bond | 0 to 100 | No | How much the partner is invested in you | Partner dumps you (Ghosted ending) |
| Chemistry | 0 to 100 | Shown as "+N" only | Avatar and outfit vs partner's Taste, upkeep | Lowers Bond gains |
| Red Flags Shown / Missed | count | No | Each tell the partner shows; "missed" if you chose to ignore or excuse it | Shown on the card |
| Goal Progress | 0 to 100 | No | Choices that move you toward your secret goal | Drives the card's goal verdict |
| Exposure | 0 to 100 | No | Risky moves that could leak (photos, Parlour posts, sneaking) | High Exposure triggers scandal cards |

Meters animate on every choice, with a floating delta ("-₦85,000", "+15 Clout") so every tap feels like a hit.

### Crossovers and cameos

Characters appear in each other's runs, so the world feels shared. Mercy can show up in Chief's run as a rival; Chief's wife can appear in Mercy's; Blessing can book Kayode's table. ### Tells

Every hidden truth has 3 written tells that appear inside cards ("his second phone buzzes face-down", "she says 'my people' when you ask about her weekend"). Careful players can read them; the Clue Pass gives one early. Each tell the player excused or ignored counts as a Red Flag Missed.

### Green flag runs

Not every relationship is trouble. About 1 in 4 runs rolls a Good One: the partner is genuinely solid, and the only real threat is you. Temptations, jealousy, your Bestie's toxic advice, a snooped phone, a Parlour leak. Every card still offers drama, and players who choose it can spoil a good thing with their own hands. This is the quiet lesson inside the chaos: sometimes the wahala is you. Players never know which kind of run they're in until the card reveals it.

## Run structure and card engine

A run is 7 days, one main card per day plus 1 or 2 surprise interrupts, finished in 3 to 5 minutes. Between cards the player can visit city spots on their in-game phone.

### The 7 day slots

1. **First Date** (character deck): the charm, the first tell.
2. **Red Flag** (character deck): something doesn't add up.
3. **Temptation** (shared city deck): an outsider or cameo makes an offer.
4. **Public Drama** (character or crossover deck): something happens in front of people.
5. **Money Trap** (character deck): a request, a deal, a sign-here moment.
6. **Confrontation** (hidden-truth deck): the truth starts leaking.
7. **Finale** (resolved from state): the ending card, then the Verdict Card.

Interrupts (work call, Bestie drama, Parlour leak, crossover rival) drop in at random on Days 2 to 6, at most 2 per run.

**Talking Time** arrives as a voice note or text between days, with a passing detail hidden inside ("Don't call me around 6pm", "I hate seafood places"). The player picks one of three replies. Caught details unlock secret options on later cards; missed ones count as Red Flags Missed or lost Chemistry. Aim for one Talking Time per day.

Days are themes, not scripts. Each slot needs at least 3 different cards so two players with the same vibe and target rarely share a Day 1. Days 2 to 5 shuffle order per run, so the Money Trap can hit on Day 2 for one player and Day 5 for another.

### Why no two runs match

Several layers stack on every run, so two players with the same vibe and target still split:

- **Hidden truth:** 1 of 3 rolled at match time (or a Good One run), and it changes the outcome tables of every card.
- **Deck shuffle:** each slot draws 1 card from 3 or more written options; Days 2 to 5 shuffle order.
- **Dice:** risky choices roll weighted outcomes (for example 50% clean, 30% partial blow-up, 20% full disaster).
- **Vibe:** sets the partner's first attitude and unlocks one vibe-only choice on many cards.
- **Chemistry:** avatar, outfit and Taste change how warmly the partner responds; Wildcard Spark can override it.
- **Talking Time:** what the player caught or missed unlocks or locks options later.
- **Secret goal:** the same ending reads as a win for one player and a roast for another.

### Work layer

Work is background, never the main game. Each vibe has a job that pays a small daily salary and throws 1 or 2 work cards per run, always colliding with the relationship: "Your MD calls an emergency meeting the night Chief finally invites you to Ikoyi." Choosing love over work can cost a promotion or pay; choosing work can cost Bond. Work outcomes show on the Verdict Card only when they mattered ("Lost a promotion for a man with a wife").

| Sapa Eviction | Wallet hits 0 | Locked out of the flat he "bought" you |

Its titles: Fumbled a Good Thing · Had Gold, Chose Gist · Your Own Red Flag.

## Viral and social layer

The Parlour makes the game feel full on day one, even before real players date each other.

### The Parlour (live feed, always one tap away)

- **Live counters:** "312 people are dating Chief right now. 41 have been ghosted today."
- **Auto-gossip:** every finished run posts an anonymous one-liner ("Someone just caught Kayode running from a raid. Left her with a ₦600k tab.").
- **Leak a Receipt:** mid-run, the player can post an anonymised moment from their run. Others react with fire, laugh, red flag or clown.
- **Rivalry alerts:** when another player is dating the same character, you get "User_Sola is also in an entanglement with Chief." Tap to see her avatar, her Clout, and what she's wearing. Choose to ignore her, track her (costs in-game ₦), or send a message through the Parlour.
- **Hall of Shame and Hall of Fame:** the day's rarest endings and funniest cards, pinned at the top.

### Built-in invite loops

1. Every card shared carries "Date [character] yourself" and "Beat their score."
2. **Squad Run:** invite up to 3 friends to date the same character at the same time; when all finish, a group card compares everyone ("Ada got the ring. Tolu got evicted. Bisi got arrested.").
3. **Invite = free Bail-Out:** one friend who finishes a run earns you one free Bail-Out.
4. **Phase 2 cliffhanger:** "Drop your WhatsApp number and we'll tell you the moment Phase 2 opens."

### Real player-to-player dating

Not in tonight's build. The Parlour, rivalry alerts and Squad Runs lay the ground. Once daily players pass a threshold you set, add matching between real players, shared runs, and in-game weddings others can attend or crash.

## Tone guide and sample cards

Write like the funniest, most savage person in the group chat who has seen everything in Lagos dating.

### Rules for every card

- Real places and real Lagos texture: Danfo, Third Mainland, Quilox, Shoprite Lekki, owambe, OPay alerts, NEPA, generator fuel.
- Pidgin and Lagos slang where a real person would use it; never forced.
- Under 45 words of setup; choices under 12 words each.
- Every choice is tempting. No obvious "good" option; the smart choice should cost something.
- Outcomes are specific and quotable ("He sent ₦50k and a voice note of him praying for you").
- Raw, messy, sexual tension and betrayal are welcome for 18+. No moral lecture; the lesson lives only on the Verdict Card.
- Every character card contains at least one tell tied to the rolled hidden truth.

### Samples

## Engine rules v2 (these override anything above that conflicts)

1. **Story memory.** Every choice can set flags (for example `took_photo_d1`, `promised_secrecy`, `lied_about_location`, `saw_second_phone`). Cards can require, exclude, or change based on flags. Each character needs at least 2 payoff cards where an earlier choice comes back. Example: you secretly photograph Chief's Rolex on Day 1; on Day 5 someone recognises the watch in your status; on Day 6 a woman messages you, and depending on the hidden truth she is his wife, his ex-wife, or a rival trying to ruin him.
2. **Relationship health is two hidden values, not one.** Attachment (how hooked the partner is) and Trust (how much they believe you). Each character has a Temperament that decides how they react: a controlling partner with high Attachment becomes more possessive, not nicer. Endings read both values.
3. **Characters remember.** Each character has 3 to 4 behaviour rules applied all run. Examples: Chief punishes public embarrassment; Tiwa cools fast if funding stops; a Good One gets hurt and withdraws after repeated accusations; Ovie gets jealous when you go to the Club.
4. **Truth roll.** On match: 25% Good One (the fake-out truth: the signature catch is a misunderstanding and the partner is faithful); 75% split evenly between the two flawed truths. Wildcard Spark (Chemistry override) is a separate roll. See rule 14 for how Good Ones behave.
5. **Phase 1 endings.** The Altar is replaced by Locked In. Marriage, family and the ring belong to later Phases.
6. **Content matrix per character (minimum):** First Date 3 · Red Flag 3 · Money Trap 3 · Confrontation 1 per truth (3) · Payoff cards 2 · Talking Time 3 = 17. Shared deck: Temptation 6 · Public Drama and crossovers 6 · Interrupts 8. A validation script runs at build time and fails if any character, slot, or truth is below the minimum or any flag is set but never paid off.
7. **Economy.** Costs scale to the player's starting Wallet in tiers; unaffordable options are greyed out with a funny reason. A "Loan App" option lets the player go negative at a brutal cost (debt collectors call your contacts on a later card). Every vibe must be able to reach Day 7. Add an automated simulation that plays 1,000 random runs per vibe and reports reach-Day-7 rates and ending spread.
8. **City spots are decisions, not stat shops.** The Bestie's intel can be wrong and the player decides whether to believe it. The Club starts a 2-day mini-storyline. The Therapist can name a manipulation tactic, which unlocks a "call it out" choice on a later card.
9. **Wahala Receipts.** The engine logs every choice. The Verdict Card includes a 4 to 5 line timeline of the most consequential moments from the actual run ("Day 2: You ignored his second phone. Day 4: You sent ₦150,000. Day 6: His wife sent you a voice note."), ending on the title and a one-line roast ("You saw the signs. You sponsored the signs."). Receipts can be shared as their own image.
10. **Server trust.** The game engine can run in the browser, but purchases, Parlour posts, counters and rarity stats are validated by the backend.
11. **Parlour honesty and safety.** Counters show real numbers only. Before there is traffic, show in-world NPC gossip, clearly as gossip, not fake player counts. Rivalry alerts show avatar and Clout; players can react with preset reactions only, with no free-text messages between players at launch. Every post has report and block; display names are filtered for slurs and real names of public figures.
12. **Share links.** Every Verdict Card and Receipt gets a public URL with an Open Graph image, tested inside WhatsApp and X before launch.
13. **Who knows what.** Every flag records who knows it: `player_knows`, `partner_knows`, `world_knows` (Parlour, friends, the wife). A character may only react to something they know or could plausibly have found out through a card that told them. The validation script fails any card that makes a character react to a fact they can't know.
14. **Good Ones are faithful, not perfect.** A Good One can still reply late, be bad with money, be moody, or keep an embarrassing secret that isn't betrayal. Their tells are designed to look suspicious. Player choices are tagged `cautious` (asking directly, verifying once, setting a boundary) or `toxic` (repeat snooping, public accusations, leaking unverified gist, revenge moves). Cautious choices never count against the player, on any run. Only `toxic` choices drain Trust on a Good One run and lead to Fumbled It. The player should genuinely be unable to tell a Good One from a convincing liar until the card reveals it.
15. **Receipts tell the truth.** Every receipt line is generated from a logged event in that run. The game never claims a warning the player didn't see, money they didn't spend, or a choice they didn't make. When a bad outcome came from a dice roll, the receipt says so ("Day 4: You played it safe. The dice said no."). Receipts get their own designed layout, as polished as the Verdict Card.
16. **Obsession ending.** A flawed partner with a controlling Temperament who reaches max Attachment triggers the Obsession ending. It is the one card that does not roast the player: no clown title, only "This one isn't funny. Control isn't love." Budget tiers (for example a ₦15k Mainland lash tech with a 40% chance of disaster) exist beside every luxury option.
17. **Launch gate.** A character only ships when it passes validation: full content matrix, every flag paid off, every ending reachable in simulation, valid probabilities (each choice's outcomes sum to 1), no negative balance without the Loan App. Target all 10 for launch; if time runs short, launch with the characters that pass (at least 3 men and 3 women) and release the rest as daily drops.
18. **Knowledge propagation.** `world_knows` never implies `partner_knows`. A partner can only confront the player after `partner_knows` is true or a discovery card has delivered the information to them. Side characters (Chief's wife, Bestie, the Amebo) can hold their own knowledge where a card needs it.
19. **Context decides cautious vs toxic.** The same action changes meaning with evidence, repetition and boundaries. Checking once after a real tell is cautious; checking again without new evidence, or crossing a stated boundary, becomes toxic. The engine tracks investigation count and evidence seen. Cautious choices don't damage Trust, but they aren't automatically rewarded either; they can cause awkwardness or lower Attachment.
20. **Receipt provenance.** Every logged event stores day, card, choice, actual outcome, money delta, cause (`chosen`, `tricked`, `dice`) and whether randomness decided it. Receipts are built from these records in order, with no invented cause and effect.
21. **Agency under control.** Max Attachment never forces an ending on its own. When a controlling partner escalates, the player always gets real exits: set a boundary, call the Bestie, leave, get help. The Obsession ending only happens if the player keeps choosing to stay. The game shows control as frightening, never as romance.
22. **Debt and dead ends.** Wallet and Debt are tracked separately. Every game state, including zero Wallet, must offer at least one choice the player can afford. Validation checks this.
23. **Testing that actually proves coverage.** Keep the random simulations, and add seeded scripted runs that deliberately reach every ending, every hidden truth, every Good One path, every flag payoff and every money edge case. Replaying the same seed with the same choices must give the same result. Report real results and failures, and never claim random runs prove full coverage. Public stats, referral rewards and paid benefits are checked against server records.

## Immersion requirements (acceptance criteria, not polish)

Wahala must never feel like a questionnaire, survey or text quiz. It plays like an interactive visual-novel scene inside a living phone: think Episode or Choices, crossed with BitLife's speed, with Wahala's own look.

1. **Screen layout during a run.** Floating HUD on top (Wallet, Sanity, Clout, animated). Scene stage in the upper part: an illustrated Lagos location background, the partner's character art with a changing expression, and speech bubbles that pop in with typing delays. The player is first-person (never drawn mid-scene). Choices appear as large tactile quick-reply bubbles (min 48px touch targets, press animation), never as a form or a list of radio buttons.
2. **The living phone.** Texts, voice notes (waveform, play button), incoming calls, bank alerts, Bestie calls, Parlour gossip and Talking Time arrive as phone notifications that slide in because something happened in the story. The phone can be opened at any time to visit city spots and the Parlour.
3. **Characters feel alive.** Each character has at least 4 expression states (for example charming, suspicious, angry, caught) that change with the scene and the player's choices. 4. **Locations have identity.** Each of the 8 launch locations listed in "Art files and launch locations (final)" has its own background art, colour mood and transition.

6. **Colour.** Base #08090D. Emerald #10B981 (money), hot pink #F43F5E (Clout), crimson #EF4444 (red flags, Sanity hits), champagne gold #F59E0B (luxury, Chemistry, rare endings). Premium, not neon soup.

## How Wahala beats Lagos Life (requirements)

Based on the research in LAGOS_LIFE_RESEARCH.md. Wahala must never look like a copy; it must feel like the upgrade.

### Design rules (apply to every scene from now on)

1. **Show, don't narrate.** Anything the story mentions must appear on screen as art or an object: the folder, the guarantor form, the tow truck and the Prado, the noodles photo, the traffic jam, the girl with the folder. If it can't be shown, cut the line.
2. **Short text.** No more than about 25 words on screen at once. Narration paragraphs are replaced by action, animation, sound and voice.
3. **Voice.** Every line a character says is voiced, using AI text-to-speech with a distinct Nigerian voice per character (Chief: deep, slow, Igbo-accented English with pidgin). Voice notes play the character's real voice with a caption, not a transcript. Build a voice pipeline that pre-generates audio files from the card data at build time; the creator can swap voices later.
4. **Different actions, not just three buttons.** Each day uses at least 3 of these interaction types, and the same type never appears twice in a row:
   - **Dress up:** wardrobe screen; drag or tap outfits, hair, shoes, bag and perfume onto your avatar and see it change.
   - **Inspect:** a zoomable object (document, phone, receipt, briefcase, photo) where the player taps to find clues, flips pages, and discovers or misses a detail.
   - **Phone:** open real-looking chats, scroll a photo, zoom on a picture someone sent, decide to reply, ignore or forward.
   - **Quick reaction:** a sudden event with a 3 to 5 second window to react (his wife walks in, the waiter brings a declined card, sirens).
   - **Place and people:** tap where to sit, who to talk to, or what to overhear in a busy venue.
   - **Haggle and pay:** a slider or tap-to-count moment for bargaining, splitting bills, or "something for the boys", with the real cost shown before paying.
   - **Talk:** a short voiced conversation with 2 or 3 tappable reply bubbles (still used, but no longer the only action).
5. **Characters perform.** Characters walk in and out, sit, gesture, look at their phone, hesitate, and change expression mid-line. Minimum 6 expressions per character (charming, laughing, suspicious, angry, caught, tender) plus at least 2 poses (standing, seated). Use layered 2D animation (subtle motion, blinking, mouth movement while voiced lines play).
6. **Busy, varied sound.** Each location layers several sounds: crowd chatter, cutlery and music in the restaurant; horns, conductors and generators on the street. No single looping ambience. Rain only when it's raining in the story.
7. **Every number must add up and be explained.** When money, Sanity or Clout changes, a short pop-up says why ("Chief sent ₦20,000 for 'your long day'"). The amount in the text, the bank alert and the meter must always match. On the Verdict Card, show money in, money out and net so they reconcile, and explain why a goal failed.
8. **Day endings are different every day.** Replace the repeated "Day survived" screen with a short recap of that day's 2 or 3 biggest moments and what they changed, then a teaser of tomorrow.
9. **City spots are short playable moments:** Glow Up changes your look on screen; the Club is a scene with people; the Bestie is a voiced call.

### The new first date (vertical slice and acceptance test)

1. Getting ready at home: dress-up wardrobe with your avatar changing on screen; the Bestie calls while you dress ("Babe, are you sure about this Chief?").
2. The ride: Chief's driver Musa arrives; a short animated ride across the Link Bridge.
3. The restaurant (Ikoyi, off Bourdillon Road): live ambience, waiters moving, music. Chief walks in, speaks, compliments (or doesn't) your outfit based on Chemistry.
4. An inspect moment: Chief steps away for a call and leaves his briefcase; a document sticks out. The player can tap to look before he returns, or not.
5. A phone moment: an unknown number sends a photo of Chief with a woman. Open, zoom, ignore or confront.
6. A quick reaction: a woman walks in (wife, ex-wife or business partner depending on the hidden truth); 4 seconds to react.
7. Consequences carried into Day 2, with a recap screen showing what changed and why.
8. Voices, sound and animation throughout; no more than 25 words on screen at a time.

**Acceptance test:** the creator plays the date on her phone. If it feels like reading and clicking, it fails. If she wants to play it again to see what happens differently, it passes. Record a screen video of the date for review.

### Adrenaline: action set pieces (part of the overhaul)

The player must feel they are doing things with their fingers, not reading. Each run includes at least 2 short action set pieces (20 to 60 seconds), built as real touch mini-games with sound, animation and a timer:

1. **Wife Escape.** The wife (or ex-wife, or business partner, per hidden truth) walks into the venue. Swipe to move, tap to hide (behind the bar, the kitchen door, the restroom), race a timer. Her tactics vary per run: a bodyguard at the back exit, a friend filming, a waiter she has paid. What she already knows depends on earlier flags (photos posted, the family WhatsApp group). Escaping, being caught, or choosing to face her all lead to different consequences.
2. **Okada Dash.** Late for a date: swipe left and right through Third Mainland Bridge traffic on an okada, dodging danfos and LASTMA, collecting time. Arrive late or on time; Chief notices.
3. **Catch the Lie.** Chief's story and his photos or documents don't match. Spot the difference (the date on a receipt, the ring mark on his finger) before he notices you looking.
4. **The Moment.** Romantic beats are physical: hold to hug, a timed tap to land the perfect compliment, choose where to sit close. Evening scenes include getting ready (including lingerie for the night), kept sensual and tasteful, never explicit.

Reactions arrive as pop-ups, sounds and animation, not instruction text: the phone buzzes and slides up, a gasp, a camera flash, a glass breaking. Players react without being told what to do.

**Variety:** set pieces, the wife and every major event can land on different days for different players. The wife may appear on any day from 2 to 6, or not at all, and behaves differently depending on the player's vibe, Chemistry and earlier choices.

### Voice plan

Characters' voices are core to the experience and are never sold separately. Generic robotic AI voices are not acceptable. Plan: record real Nigerian voices once, about 40 short clips per character ("Ehen!", "My darling", "Who told you?", "Sit down.", laughs, sighs), and reuse them across all runs, with captions for full lines. Store clips in `public/audio/voices/<character>/<clip>.mp3` and reference them from card data. Until clips are recorded, show captions with expressive sound effects (no robotic text-to-speech).

## Live Spots: real players together (Phase 1, overrides rule 11's "no free-text messages at launch")

Wahala must never feel like playing alone. Lagos Life's pull is thousands of real players sharing one Lagos. Wahala adds that from Phase 1.

1. **Spots are live venues** using real Lagos places: a VIP lounge on Ozumba Mbadiwe, a beach bar at Elegushi, a café on Admiralty Way. Players enter between days (or anytime from the phone) and see other real players there as their avatars, moving and idling.
2. **Who's here:** each player shows a display name, avatar and a badge of their current run ("Dating Chief · Day 4"). Tap someone to see their Clout and their latest shared Verdict title.
3. **Things to do together:** text chat, emoji reactions, buy someone a drink with game money, dance, toast, invite to a Squad Run, compare runs with others dating the same character, start a rivalry.

5. **AI and real together:** the AI Bestie and NPC regulars (bartender, gossip aunty, DJ) keep Spots lively when few players are online. They are always labelled as characters, never presented as real people.

## Wahala World: the dating world design (direction for Live City and Live Spots; build after the first date passes)

**Identity:** Wahala is a living virtual dating world. Lagos Life is a life simulator where dating is one activity; in Wahala, every place, activity and system exists for love, attraction, relationships and drama. Work, shopping and transport appear only where they feed dating.

### Two kinds of dating, one avatar, one city

1. **Fictional dating (the drama):** Chief Emeka and the roster, with hidden truths, set pieces and Verdict Cards.
2. **Real-player dating (the unexpected):** real players meet through avatars, talk, flirt, go on dates, and become friends, rivals or virtual couples. Nothing scripted.
3. **Collisions:** Chief texts while you're flirting at the lounge; your real friends react; someone says they dated a completely different Chief.

### Status and privacy (overrides the earlier "Dating Chief · Day 4" badge)

Players choose their visible status: Single, Open to Dates, Taken (virtual), Here for Friends, or Private. Fictional romances stay private unless the player shares them. Usernames and avatars only; no real-world identity required.

Each venue has its own crowd of NPC regulars, its own activities, and its own kind of romance. Appearances can deceive (the hotel "billionaire" who isn't paying for his room).

| --- | --- | --- |
| Dating lounge, Ozumba Mbadiwe, Victoria Island (the heartbeat) | Everyone looking | Mingle, dance, buy drinks, private booths by mutual consent, Speed Dating Nights, Wahala Table |

### Real-player dating features

1. **Date invitations:** choose place, activity and transport; the other player accepts, declines or suggests another place. Then both go and do it together.

Real-player romance is optional and consensual. Private booths, voice calls and dates require mutual acceptance. Blocking ends all contact and visibility. No following after a block, no sharing personal information, phone numbers or links. Reporting and moderation operate from launch.

## Playtest 2 verdict and 3D proof scene (TOP PRIORITY; overrides 2D scene presentation everywhere)

**Creator's playtest of the new first date:** "It is still options to click and text to read. Code alone can only draw shapes. Living characters need 3D models with rigs and animation files. Use ready-made, properly licensed assets now; commissioned Wahala art replaces them later.

1. **Engine:** Three.js (with glTF assets and an animation mixer) in the existing PWA. Mid-range Android performance is a requirement.
2. **Characters:** rigged, stylized (not blocky) humanoid models with animation clips for idle, walk, run, sit down, sit idle, stand up, raise glass and drink, talk gestures, wave, hug, dance, hold phone, carry bag.
